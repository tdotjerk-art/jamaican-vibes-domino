Game activity

    Jamaican 3 Cards

    Use a regular 52-card pack. Everyone picks a card; highest deals. Ace is high in this version. Tied highest players pick again.

    1. The dealer shuffles, deals 3 cards each, then turns up a trump card. A 6 or Jack gives the dealer 1 point per player (3 players = 3 points). Play continues.
    2. Before anyone plays a card, every player must declare their strongest qualifying call or pass. Only the strongest declared hand receives call points. Computers declare qualifying hands automatically. Calls are settled once, before matching-card play starts; playing continues for the separate winning point.
    3. Calls, strongest first: Flush, straight and threes — 3 points each: three cards of the same rank beats two Jacks and a six, which beats two sixes and a Jack. Higher three-of-a-kind beats lower. This is the Jamaican special category name; it does not require the cards to form a flush or a straight. Flush and straight — 2 points each: three consecutive cards of one suit. Flush — 1 point each: three cards of one suit. Straight — 1 point each: three consecutive ranks.
    4. For the same call, compare the highest card, then the second, then the third. Ace is high except in A–2–3, the lowest straight; Q–K–A is the highest straight. K–A–2 is not a straight. Suits do not break ties.
    5. Each other player pays the strongest caller the call’s point value: at a four-player table, a three-point call gives the winner +9 and each opponent −3. If the strongest declared hands tie exactly, the non-winning players each pay the call value and the tied winners share that pool equally. For example, two tied three-point callers get +3 each, and the other two players lose 3 each. Equal shares can include fractions. If everyone ties, no points move.
    6. Starting to the dealer’s right, a player holding the same rank can play their matching card plus another card from their hand. The turned-up card stays on the table.
    7. The other card played becomes the next rank to match. Chances to use it pass to the right. You can decline.
    8. With a pair and one different card (for example 2, 2, King), use a matching table card for that different rank to win. Match the different card to win and clear all three cards, including the pair. Your chance must come in rotation, including after someone declines a drawn card.
    9. With just one card left, use a matching table card to play your last card alone and win.
    10. If nobody uses the opening card, the second player to the dealer’s right draws. A draw turns up one card; it does not enter anyone’s hand. The drawer has first choice, then chances pass to the right. If nobody uses it, the next player draws.
    11. After all calls are declared, the strongest caller’s cards are shown face up at their seat for everyone to see; tied strongest callers each show their cards. When a player wins the matching-card hand, their finishing cards are shown face up in front of them with VICTORY until the next game.
    12. The matching-card hand winner gets +1. Everyone else gets −1. These hand points are separate from calls and dealer bonuses. The winner deals next.
    13. If the deck runs out, no hand points change, but all call points and the dealer’s bonus are kept. The game counts; everyone picks again to select a dealer.

    Agree on 5, 10, 15 games or more. At the end, positive scores win; negative scores lose; zero is even. Add more games in steps of 5 by agreement.

    Two, three, or four players can play. For computer play, choose the total number of players: you plus one, two, or three computers. Online, the host can start with two to four connected players once everyone chooses the same game count and taps Ready. Online play pauses if someone disconnects.
