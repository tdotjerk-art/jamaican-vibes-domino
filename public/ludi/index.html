    How to play
    1. Each player has four pieces. Roll a six to move a piece from its yard to its starting square. Green arrows mark the starting squares.
    2. Move around the track in the arrow direction, then up your own home lane. Roll both dice on the table, then choose a piece for the highlighted die. Tap either die to choose which one to play first, including the smaller die. Use both dice whenever the required order allows two legal moves. Move the same piece twice or split the dice between two pieces. You cannot choose a capture or other move that wastes the second die when a two-move sequence is available. You need the exact roll to finish.
    3. There are no safe zones on the shared track. Landing on a lone opponent sends that piece back to its yard, including on arrow starting squares. Entering from your yard also captures a lone opponent on your starting square. A piece that captures stops for the rest of that roll: use the remaining die on a different piece. A capture is not allowed if stopping that piece would discard an otherwise playable second die. Another piece must use it, unless a barrier genuinely blocks the remaining move. Skip an unusable die only when no legal sequence can use both, such as when a barrier blocks the move. Capturing pieces become available again on the next roll.
    4. Two or more of your pieces on one outer-track square form a barrier. Opponents cannot land on it, capture its pieces, or pass it, including on starting squares. You may stack all four pieces together and pass your own barrier. A barrier stays protected while at least two pieces remain; a lone piece can be captured.
    5. Only a six on either die earns another roll after you use the available dice. Capturing an opponent or bringing a piece home does not earn an extra roll. There is no limit on consecutive rolls containing a six; repeated sixes do not forfeit your turn. If neither die can move a piece, your turn passes automatically. A die that cannot be used is skipped.
    6. Bring all four pieces home to win. Two-player games use opposite corners. No teams: everyone plays for themselves.

    Computer turns pause so you can follow the game. Online games require 2–4 players. Public tables fill in arrival order and start when full. Stay on the waiting screen or cancel your search; private rooms use separate invite codes. All players must consent to a new game. Your name is remembered on this device; your saved room token recovers your seat. Rooms last up to 24 hours and are saved across server restarts and deploys. Each human action has a 10-second countdown; online clocks follow the server. Computer turns use shorter pauses. A connection badge shows when you are reconnecting or back online. At the end, request a rematch and see who is ready.
