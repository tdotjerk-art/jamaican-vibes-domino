        Hand recap

          How to play

          Make the best five-card poker hand using any combination of your two private cards and the five community cards.

          Eight seats: you and seven computer opponents. Each player starts with 1,000 play chips. The dealer button rotates. Starting blinds: 10 / 20. Tournament blinds double every five hands. Betting rounds: preflop, flop (3 cards), turn (1), river (1).

          Check when no chips are owed, call to match, raise to increase the total bet, or fold. The minimum full raise is the size of the previous full raise. A short all-in does not reopen raising unless the cumulative increase is a full raise.

          Strongest first: straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, pair, high card. Aces can be low in A–2–3–4–5. Suits never break ties. Equal hands split the pot; odd chips go clockwise after the dealer. All-ins create side pots, awarded only to eligible players.

          Opponent cards are revealed at showdown. If everybody else folds, the winner’s private cards remain hidden. Busted seats sit out; the table ends when one player has all the chips. New table resets all chips.

          Poker hands · strongest first

          1. Royal flush — A K Q J 10, all the same suit.
          2. Straight flush — Five consecutive cards, all the same suit.
          3. Four of a kind — Four cards of one rank, plus a fifth card.
          4. Full house — Three of one rank and two of another.
          5. Flush — Five cards of one suit.
          6. Straight — Five consecutive ranks. An ace can be high or low in A 2 3 4 5.
          7. Three of a kind — Three cards of one rank, plus two other cards.
          8. Two pair — Two cards of one rank and two of another, plus a fifth card.
          9. One pair — Two cards of one rank, plus three other cards.
          10. High card — Compare the highest cards when no stronger combination is made.

          Your best five cards decide the result. A royal flush is the highest straight flush. Higher ranks win within a category. Remaining cards break ties where applicable. Equal five-card hands split the pot; suits do not break ties.
